# Stakeholder and Decision Matrix

This artifact extends the dissertation into an operating decision context. The stakeholder roles below are inferred from the dissertation's deployment concept and service recommendations. They were not validated through stakeholder interviews.

| Stakeholder | Primary need | Decision supported | Evidence/input required | Output expected |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Programme leadership | Identify recurring student experience issues across periods and modules | Which service areas require intervention or closer review | Sentiment distribution, recurring themes, period/module context, response coverage | Prioritized improvement areas and escalation list |
| Module leaders / teaching teams | Understand specific strengths and weaknesses in delivery | Which teaching or course-design practices should be retained, changed, or tested | Theme-level feedback examples, recurring concerns, positive practices | Targeted teaching or course-design action |
| Student experience / quality team | Monitor whether issues are persistent rather than isolated | Which themes should be tracked across reporting cycles | Multi-semester trends, repeated themes, issue status | Recurring issue register and follow-up recommendations |
| Academic leadership | Compare themes across broader service areas | Where standardized guidance or cross-module support may be useful | Aggregated patterns across modules and periods | Cross-cutting improvement priorities |
| Reporting / analysis owner | Maintain a reliable and repeatable feedback process | Whether data are sufficiently complete and interpretable for reporting | Data quality checks, classification validation, exception cases | Analysis-ready dataset, flagged exceptions, reporting pack |
| Students | Provide the underlying experience signal | Indirectly influence which areas are reviewed and improved | Open-ended feedback and response coverage | Improved learning and support experience |

## Decision hierarchy

The analysis supports three levels of decision-making:

1. **Operational review:** Which comments or themes require immediate human attention?
2. **Service improvement:** Which recurring issues justify changes to teaching, course structure, practical learning, or student interaction?
3. **Performance monitoring:** Did the selected intervention reduce the targeted issue in a later reporting cycle?

## Key decision principle

A classification result should not be treated as the decision itself. It is an input used to organize feedback, surface recurring issues, and direct attention. Ambiguous, neutral, or indirectly negative feedback should remain reviewable in context before action is taken.
