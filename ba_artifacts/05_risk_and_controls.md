# Risk and Control Framework

This framework translates limitations and error patterns identified in the dissertation into operational controls for a recurring feedback-reporting process.

| Risk | How it could affect a decision | Proposed control | Monitoring trigger | Likely owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Indirect negative feedback is classified as neutral or positive | Material complaints may not be escalated | Maintain manual review for ambiguous, low-confidence, or complaint-like neutral comments | Increase in reviewed cases where final interpretation differs from initial category | Reporting / analysis owner |
| Neutral feedback contains suggestions that are treated as low priority | Constructive improvement opportunities may be overlooked | Report neutral themes separately rather than combining them with positive feedback | Repeated suggestion themes across periods | Programme or quality team |
| Uneven response rates across modules or semesters | High or low sentiment may be over-interpreted as representative | Show response volume and coverage context beside findings | Material change in response coverage or very low module participation | Reporting owner / programme team |
| Duplicate or low-information responses enter the reporting population | Sentiment and theme counts may be distorted | Apply documented exclusion rules and report excluded-record counts | Unexpected change in retention rate or duplicate rate | Reporting / analysis owner |
| Theme meaning changes over time | Historical classification patterns may become less relevant | Re-review error patterns and examples each reporting cycle | Decline in later-period validation or emergence of new phrasing | Reporting / analysis owner |
| Aggregated reporting removes context | Decision-makers may act on a theme without understanding the underlying issue | Preserve traceability from theme to authorized source-comment review | Stakeholder challenge or conflicting interpretation | Reporting owner |
| Recommendations have no accountable owner | Findings may not lead to change | Assign each accepted improvement action to a stakeholder group and review status in the next cycle | Repeated issue with no recorded action | Programme leadership |
| Intervention success is inferred from model performance | Technical validation may be mistaken for evidence of service improvement | Separate classification metrics from service outcome measures | Reporting pack contains model scores without post-intervention measures | Programme leadership / reporting owner |
| Single-institution evidence is generalized too broadly | Recommendations may be applied outside the context supported by the data | State scope explicitly and validate locally before broader rollout | Expansion to another programme, faculty, or institution | Decision owner |
| Institutional feedback is exposed publicly | Privacy or data-use obligations may be breached | Keep original feedback and labeled derivatives out of the public repository | Any request to publish source-level institutional text | Data owner |

## Control design principle

The highest-risk failure is not a lower headline accuracy score. It is a **decision-relevant error**, such as missing a genuine complaint, overstating a trend from weak participation, or recommending an action without sufficient context.

The control framework therefore combines data-quality checks, contextual review, traceability, clear ownership, and post-action measurement.
